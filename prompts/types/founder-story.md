# Founder story

**Goal:** make people care about the person and the problem before they care about the features. Best for the landing page, an About page, a launch thread and a first investor email.

## Structure and motion
1. **The moment (3 s)** `title`: the situation that started it ("Our invoices lived in 4 spreadsheets"). One concrete detail beats a big claim.
2. **The frustration (4 s)** `quote` or `title`: what was broken and why nothing on the market fixed it, in the founder's own words when the sources have them.
3. **The insight (4 s)** `title`: the one idea that changed the approach ("What if the pipeline was the only screen?").
4. **The product (4-5 s)** `screenshot`: the product doing the thing that came out of that insight, with a 2-4 word caption.
5. **Today (3 s, only if real)** `stats`: users, repos, stars or years building, only numbers in the sources.
6. **Outro (3 s)** `outro`: a human call to action ("Say hi", "Try it free"), with the URL.

## In each style
- **Clean:** calm and sincere, slow pushes, one line at a time.
- **Clay:** warm and personal; the title pops like a sticker on a desk.
- **Glass:** cinematic origin trailer; the insight line lands after a held beat of glow.
- **Isometric:** the problem is a tangled stack of blocks, the insight tidies them into one.
- **Hand-drawn:** a diary page: scribbled problem, a circled insight, a drawn arrow to the product. The most personal look.
- **ASCII:** a commit log: `git log` lines with the dates of the struggle, then `> fixed`.

## Rules
- Use only what the sources say about how and why the product began: the About page, a founder blog post, the README. Never invent a backstory, a quote or a customer.
- Write in first person plural ("we") or singular only if the sources do. No hero worship, no "journey", no "passion".
- If the sources contain no origin story, do not fabricate one: keep the problem-first structure and say what the product replaces.
