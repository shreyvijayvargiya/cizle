Most bad AI video comes from a vague prompt, not a weak model. "Make a cool video of a product" leaves the model to guess the framing, the light, the speed and the mood. Here is a way to write prompts that remove the guessing.

## The formula

Write the prompt in this order and keep each part short:

1. **Camera**: one named move, such as a slow dolly in, an orbit, a locked-off shot or a handheld follow.
2. **Subject and setting**: who or what is on screen, and where.
3. **Action**: one thing that happens.
4. **Look**: light, colour and texture.
5. **Timing**: how long the clip runs and how the motion eases (slow, snappy, held at the end).

Runway's prompting guide suggests leading with the camera so the model frames everything else around it, and recommends one action per prompt, because most tools produce clips of roughly 5 to 10 seconds ([Runway](https://runwayml.com/resources/ai-video-prompting-guide)).

## One camera move per clip

Luma advises a single camera move per clip and cutting several clips together in editing if you need more ([Luma](https://lumalabs.ai/news/write-ai-video-prompts)). "Slow push in, then pan left" tends to confuse the model. Use filmmaking words (dolly, tracking, tilt, crane, orbit): Kapwing's camera guide says specific terms instruct the model far better than "dynamic camera movement" ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/the-complete-ai-camera-prompt-guide-77-shots-and-movements/)).

## Say what you want, not what to avoid

Runway also notes that "no camera shake" can make the model focus on the shake. Describe the look you want instead: "steady, locked-off frame".

## Before and after

**Before:** make a cool video of a product

**After:** 9:16, 8 seconds. Near-black stage with one teal rim light. The box opens and the product rises and turns to face the camera. The headline types in below it. Slow eased motion, and hold the last frame.

The second prompt names the format, the light, the single action, the pacing and the ending. Nothing is left to chance.

## Reuse prompts that already worked

You do not have to start from a blank page. A prompt library is a collection of prompts that already produced a result you liked, so you can copy one and change the subject. On cizle every prompt credits its creator, and video prompts play their demo so you can see the result before you copy the text. 20 prompts are free.

## A checklist before you generate

- Is there exactly one camera move?
- Is there exactly one action?
- Did you name the light and the colours?
- Did you say how long it runs and how it ends?
- Is the format (vertical, wide, square) stated?

If you can tick all five, the clip will usually be close on the first try.
